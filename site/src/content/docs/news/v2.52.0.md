The two uplink ports can now be one LACP link, and a BMC reboot keeps the nodes' power state as it was. LACP is off unless you turn it on. The switch chip's own trunk hardware carries the bond, and the whole LACP work is [@danste22](https://github.com/danste22)'s (Daniel): the kernel patches, the bonding, the daemon, the interface and the command line ([#79](https://github.com/excavador-turing/BMC-Firmware/pull/79)). Thank you, Daniel. The power-state fix builds on his gpio-latch patch, from an idea of Sven Rademakers.

<!-- more -->

Firmware **v2.52.0**, 21 November 2026 — the first image to carry [bmcd 2.46.0](/changelog/bmcd/), [BMC-UI 4.7.0](/changelog/bmc-ui/), [tpi 1.11.0](/changelog/tpi/). Every item below is the repository's own changelog entry, taken apart; the whole entry is a click away under each one.

## New

### Hardware LACP: ge0 and ge1 as one 802.3ad link
<small class="tp-tag">bmcd 2.46.0</small>

`ge0` and `ge1` can be one bond, `bond0`, backed by the switch chip's own trunk hardware (the RTL8370 LAG), from the Switch page or with `tpi network switch apply --preset flat-lag` (or `trunk-lag`). Nothing changes for a board that does not use it.

<details class="note">
<summary>The whole entry</summary>

LACP is off by default: without a bond in `/etc/network/interfaces` the board stays the flat `br0` it is today. When you turn it on, the change goes through the usual confirm window and is put back automatically if you do not confirm it, and also if the uplink never starts forwarding, so a bond that never negotiates cannot leave the board off the network. The bond uses the `layer2+3` hash and fast LACP, and the switch at the other end needs LACP on both ports.

[@danste22](https://github.com/danste22) tested it on a Zyxel switch: both ports up, `ge0` alone (the bond keeps its carrier), the automatic revert in both directions, and an upstream that does not speak LACP. The kernel patches are his ([#79](https://github.com/excavador-turing/BMC-Firmware/pull/79), carried on as [#87](https://github.com/excavador-turing/BMC-Firmware/pull/87)), and so are the daemon ([bmcd#50](https://github.com/excavador-turing/bmcd/pull/50)), the interface ([BMC-UI#58](https://github.com/excavador-turing/BMC-UI/pull/58)) and the command line ([tpi#16](https://github.com/excavador-turing/tpi/pull/16)); each was carried on with his commits kept under his authorship. Thank you, Daniel.

</details>

### The Switch page has an LACP switch, and `tpi` has the `flat-lag` and `trunk-lag` presets
<small class="tp-tag">BMC-UI 4.7.0</small> <small class="tp-tag">tpi 1.11.0</small>

The page shows the switch only on a board that can do LACP, and marks a running bond with a badge. Over a hand-written network file, both ask before replacing it, and the board keeps a copy.

<details class="note">
<summary>The whole entry</summary>

On the command line the same is `--second-uplink lag` on `flat` or `trunk`, and `--replace-hand-written` for the file. Thanks [@danste22](https://github.com/danste22) ([BMC-UI#58](https://github.com/excavador-turing/BMC-UI/pull/58), [tpi#16](https://github.com/excavador-turing/tpi/pull/16)).

</details>

## Fixed

- **A reboot of the BMC on the 6.18 kernel is read as a warm boot again.** bmcd logged a cold boot after every reboot with all four modules running, and restored the saved power state over the live one. <small class="tp-tag">bmcd 2.46.0</small>

    <details class="note">
    <summary>The whole entry</summary>

    The daemon now reads which nodes are powered from the latch shadow word that the kernel's gpio-latch patch keeps in SoC SRAM, so a node that is running stays running, and starting the daemon no longer holds running modules in reset for a moment. A cold boot still restores the saved state. The contract is the one [@danste22](https://github.com/danste22) carried to 6.18, with the original idea from Sven Rademakers, whose 2024 patch in turing-machines/BMC-Firmware started it. Thanks to both.

    </details>

## Changed

- **bmcd 2.46.0, BMC-UI 4.7.0 and tpi 1.11.0** are the pinned versions, the latest of each.

## Known issue

An over-the-air write was seen to fail once, on one board, with a UBI write timeout. The firmware the board was running keeps booting. Power-cycle the board and retry, or flash from the SD card. `tpi-selfupdate` now cleans up the staged image after a failed write and says what to do ([BMC-Firmware#102](https://github.com/excavador-turing/BMC-Firmware/pull/102)).
