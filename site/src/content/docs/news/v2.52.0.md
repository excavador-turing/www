The two uplink ports can now be one LACP link, and a BMC reboot keeps the nodes' power state as it was. LACP is off unless you turn it on. The switch chip's own trunk hardware carries the bond, and the whole LACP work is [@danste22](https://github.com/danste22)'s (Daniel): the kernel patches, the bonding, the daemon, the interface and the command line ([#79](https://github.com/excavador-turing/BMC-Firmware/pull/79)). Thank you, Daniel. The power-state fix builds on his gpio-latch patch, from an idea of Sven Rademakers.

Firmware **v2.52.0**, 21 November 2026 — the first image to carry [bmcd 2.46.0](/changelog/bmcd/), [BMC-UI 4.7.0](/changelog/bmc-ui/), [tpi 1.11.0](/changelog/tpi/). Every item below is the repository's own changelog entry, taken apart; the whole entry is a click away under each one.

## New

### LACP uplink: `ge0` and `ge1` as one 802.3ad `bond0` on the RTL8370 hardware LAG, by @danste22 (#79, #87, bmcd#50, BMC-UI#58, tpi#16), thanks!

Three kernel patches (`net-dsa/0014` to `0016`) offload a Linux bond onto the switch chip's own trunk hardware, `CONFIG_BONDING` is built in, and the kernel command line carries `bonding.max_bonds=0`, so no `bond0` exists until ifupdown-ng creates the configured one.

<details class="note">
<summary>The whole entry</summary>

bmcd 2.46.0 creates `bond0` (802.3ad, `layer2+3` hash, fast LACP) over `ge0` and `ge1` when the switch asks for `second_uplink: lag`, and writes it into `/etc/network/interfaces` so the bond is up before the daemon starts. BMC-UI 4.7.0 has the LACP switch and the `flat-lag` and `trunk-lag` presets on the Switch page, and tpi 1.11.0 has `tpi network switch apply --preset flat-lag` (or `trunk-lag`, or `--second-uplink lag`). A change goes through the usual confirm window and is put back automatically if it is not confirmed; if the uplink never forwards after an apply, bmcd reverts anyway, 60 s after the window would have ended. A hand-written `/etc/network/interfaces` needs an explicit opt-in (the checkbox, `--replace-hand-written`, or `replace_hand_written: true`), and only when the change turns LACP on. A `bond0` that bmcd did not create is refused. The switch at the other end needs LACP on both ports. Nothing creates a bond by itself: without one in `/etc/network/interfaces` the board stays the flat `br0` it is today. `tests/lag-parts.sh` checks the kernel pieces offline. The patches keep their `From:` and `Signed-off-by:` headers.

Tested by @danste22 on a Zyxel LACP switch: both ports in one aggregator,
`ge0` alone (the bond keeps its carrier and nothing is sent to the dead
port), the automatic revert in both directions, and an upstream without LACP.
We tested it on two boards with LACP off, which is how every board runs it by
default. Each part was carried on from his pull request with his commits kept
under his authorship ([bmcd#56](https://github.com/excavador-turing/bmcd/pull/56),
[BMC-UI#60](https://github.com/excavador-turing/BMC-UI/pull/60),
[tpi#17](https://github.com/excavador-turing/tpi/pull/17)).

</details>

### LACP (`SecondUplink::Lag`) on Flat and Trunk
<small class="tp-tag">bmcd 2.45.0</small>

`bond0` (802.3ad) carries the uplink VLANs; ge0 and ge1 are empty slaves.

<details class="note">
<summary>The whole entry</summary>

The presets list shows them as `flat-lag` and `trunk-lag`; the value to send is the preset's `preset` field, for example `{"preset":"flat","second_uplink":"lag"}`. Split stays incompatible with LAG. The bond is created with `xmit_hash_policy layer2+3` and `lacp_rate fast`, live and in the `interfaces` stanza: the switch copies the bond's hash when it joins, and the kernel default `layer2` put two nodes' uploads on one member. With DHCP, `br0` gets `udhcpc-opts "-t 25 -T 2"` so the lease waits for LACP to reach Distributing. The confirm window watches `bond0`. LACP needs ifupdown-ng's bond executor on the image. (thanks [@danste22](https://github.com/danste22), [#50](https://github.com/excavador-turing/bmcd/pull/50)/[#56](https://github.com/excavador-turing/bmcd/pull/56))

</details>

### A confirmed switch change also writes `/etc/network/interfaces`
<small class="tp-tag">bmcd 2.45.0</small>

One locked writer, shared with address confirm.

<details class="note">
<summary>The whole entry</summary>

A `# bmcd-switch:` header carries the document, and `bond0`, `bridge-ports` and the `post-up` lines come from it, so a reboot brings the bond and the VLANs up before this daemon starts. (thanks [@danste22](https://github.com/danste22), [#50](https://github.com/excavador-turing/bmcd/pull/50)/[#56](https://github.com/excavador-turing/bmcd/pull/56))

</details>

### A document without `second_uplink` is read one way, whatever it came in by
<small class="tp-tag">bmcd 2.45.0</small>

The file on the overlay, the `interfaces` header and an API body all infer it from the ports and STP (a Split, or a Trunk with ge1 off, is `off`; STP on is `redundant`; `bond0` in the ports is `lag`), instead of the API defaulting to `redundant` while the file inferred.

### LACP on the Switch page
<small class="tp-tag">BMC-UI 4.7.0</small>

(thanks [@danste22](https://github.com/danste22), [#58](https://github.com/excavador-turing/BMC-UI/pull/58)).

<details class="note">
<summary>The whole entry</summary>

The board's `flat-lag` and `trunk-lag` presets are in the list, and an LACP switch beside VLAN filtering and spanning tree bundles ge0 and ge1 into `bond0`. With LACP on, ge0 and ge1 are locked as empty slaves, `bond0` carries the uplink VLANs, and spanning tree is off. The running view shows an LACP badge. The switch at the other end needs LACP on both ports, and the description says so. The draft logic moved out of `SwitchConfig.tsx` into `switchDraft.ts`, with tests.

</details>

### Replacing a hand-written network file
<small class="tp-tag">BMC-UI 4.7.0</small>

LACP needs `bond0` declared in `/etc/network/interfaces`.

<details class="note">
<summary>The whole entry</summary>

When the board answers that the file was written by hand, the page says so and shows "Replace my hand-written network file"; Apply stays off until it is ticked, then sends `replace_hand_written`. The board keeps a copy of the old file. A `bond0` the board did not create is explained rather than retried.

</details>

### The LACP switch appears only on a board that shows it can do LACP (a preset that uses it, or LACP already running), so a daemon from before bmcd's LACP support does not get a control it would refuse
<small class="tp-tag">BMC-UI 4.7.0</small>

Turning LACP off on a flat layout gives back the flat layout: spanning tree follows VLAN filtering, as in the board's presets.

### `tpi network switch` LACP uplink
<small class="tp-tag">tpi 1.11.0</small>

`--preset flat-lag` / `trunk-lag`, or `--second-uplink lag` on `flat` / `trunk`, send the board `{"preset":"flat|trunk","second_uplink":"lag"}` so ge0+ge1 become LACP `bond0`.

<details class="note">
<summary>The whole entry</summary>

`show` and `presets` now print the port table under flat+lag too, so `bond0` is visible, and say that ge0 and ge1 are the bond's members (their empty rows are not a fault). Needs a bmcd that accepts `second_uplink: lag` (and ifupdown-ng's bond executor to confirm), and LACP on both ports of the switch at the other end. (thanks [@danste22](https://github.com/danste22), [#16](https://github.com/excavador-turing/tpi/pull/16)/[#17](https://github.com/excavador-turing/tpi/pull/17))

</details>

### `tpi network switch apply --replace-hand-written`
<small class="tp-tag">tpi 1.11.0</small>

LACP needs `bond0` declared in `/etc/network/interfaces`, so over a hand-written file the board refuses it with a 409.

<details class="note">
<summary>The whole entry</summary>

With the flag the request carries `replace_hand_written: true`, and a confirmed change replaces the file (the board keeps a copy beside it). Without it, that 409 now says so in `tpi`'s words and names the flag; the 409 for a `bond0` the board did not make says how to remove it. Every other change needs neither. (review of [#16](https://github.com/excavador-turing/tpi/pull/16))

</details>


## Changed

- **bmcd 2.46.0, BMC-UI 4.7.0 and tpi 1.11.0.** Are the pinned versions (the latest of each).
##### Known issue

- **One over-the-air write was seen to fail, on one board.** Writing the image with `tpi-selfupdate` failed with a UBI write timeout on the final block (`ubiupdatevol: error 110`). The firmware the board was running is untouched and still boots.

    <details class="note">
    <summary>The whole entry</summary>

    Power-cycle the board, then retry or flash from the SD card (`tpi-selfupdate` now says so itself). The cause is not found yet.

    </details>

##### Tests

- **`tests/selfupdate-failure.sh` covers the failure paths with stubbed `curl`,
`df` and `osupdate`, under dash and busybox ash; the mutation check in
`checks.yml` fails if the cleanup, the advice or the writability test is
removed.**
- **Upgrading rewrites an address-only `interfaces` once.** A board whose `interfaces` was written by an earlier bmcd as the address alone has it rewritten at the first start of this version, now carrying the switch's own boot lines (and `bond0` when LACP is confirmed). <small class="tp-tag">bmcd 2.45.0</small>

    <details class="note">
    <summary>The whole entry</summary>

    The log line says so. A file written by hand is never rewritten by this.

    </details>

- **A hand-written `interfaces` holds up LACP only.** LACP is the one change that needs the file (`bond0` has to exist before this daemon starts), so only an apply that asks for `second_uplink: lag` over a hand-written file is refused, with a 409, unless the request carries `replace_hand_written: true` (a copy of the file is kept when the change is confirmed). <small class="tp-tag">bmcd 2.45.0</small>

    <details class="note">
    <summary>The whole entry</summary>

    Every other change is applied live and persisted in `switch.json` as before, and a hand-written file is left alone, at confirm and at boot. A hand-written file whose address cannot be read (not DHCP, not a static address with a netmask) is refused instead of turned into DHCP. The 409 names the options a client has: the interface's checkbox, `tpi --replace-hand-written`, or the field in the request; the clients grow them separately.

    </details>

- **The OpenAPI request bodies of `PUT` and `validate` on the switch are the
`SwitchPreset` and `SwitchDocument` components,.** With `window_s` and `replace_hand_written` beside them, instead of a hand-written `properties` list that had already drifted from them. <small class="tp-tag">bmcd 2.45.0</small>
- **The API types are generated from bmcd v2.45.0,.** The first daemon with LACP ([bmcd#56](https://github.com/excavador-turing/bmcd/pull/56), also [@danste22](https://github.com/danste22)'s, from [bmcd#50](https://github.com/excavador-turing/bmcd/pull/50)). <small class="tp-tag">BMC-UI 4.7.0</small>

    <details class="note">
    <summary>The whole entry</summary>

    `bmcd-release.txt` and `src/lib/api/schema.d.ts`, with `npm run api:refresh`. The hand-written switch types in `src/lib/api/get.ts` now follow the generated ones (`SecondUplink`, the port membership, `SwitchDocument`, the preset's `preset` field), so a change in the daemon's document shows up as a type error here.

    </details>

- **`--preset flat --second-uplink redundant|off` is refused.** It used to be ignored without a word; flat takes only `--second-uplink lag` (or `--preset flat-lag`). `--preset split` refuses `--second-uplink` as well. <small class="tp-tag">tpi 1.11.0</small>

    <details class="note">
    <summary>The whole entry</summary>

    (thanks [@danste22](https://github.com/danste22), [#16](https://github.com/excavador-turing/tpi/pull/16)/[#17](https://github.com/excavador-turing/tpi/pull/17))

    </details>


## Fixed

- **A reboot of the BMC on the 6.18 kernel is read as a warm boot again
(bmcd 2.46.0).** Bmcd up to 2.45.0 logged `cold boot: restoring persisted power state` after every reboot of the BMC on 6.18 (v2.51.0), with all four modules running, where on 6.12 it adopted the live state.

    <details class="note">
    <summary>The whole entry</summary>

    The saved state was then restored over the live one, so a node that was off in the database but on in fact was switched off. bmcd now reads which nodes are powered from the latch shadow word that the kernel's gpio-latch patch keeps in SoC SRAM (the cooperative contract with the bootloader that [@danste22](https://github.com/danste22) carried to 6.18, with the original idea from Sven Rademakers, whose 2024 patch in turing-machines/BMC-Firmware started it; thanks to both), and requests the node enable lines with that value, so starting the daemon no longer pulses running modules either ([bmcd#58](https://github.com/excavador-turing/bmcd/pull/58)). A cold boot still restores the persisted state. No kernel change.

    </details>

- **`tpi-selfupdate` cleans up after a failed update and says what to do
([#102](https://github.com/excavador-turing/BMC-Firmware/pull/102)).** A write that failed on the last partial LEB (`ubiupdatevol: ... error 110`) left the ~39 MB download in `/mnt/overlay`, which has very little room, and printed nothing about the state the board was in.

    <details class="note">
    <summary>The whole entry</summary>

    After a write error other than EIO UBI goes read-only until the next boot, so retrying is useless and even `tpi reboot` can fail. The download is now removed on every exit (failure, checksum mismatch, interrupted transfer, SIGINT/SIGTERM) and only that file, under a name carrying the PID so a file already in the directory is never touched. A failed write exits 1 and states that the running firmware is untouched and will boot, that UBI may be read-only, and to power-cycle the board and retry or flash from the SD card. `--keep` keeps the image only after a successful write.

    </details>

- **Staging says when it is not on the SD card.** The card was already preferred; it is now also required to be writable (a card remounted read-only after an error used to be picked and then failed), and the script names the reason when it falls back to the overlay or `/tmp`.
- **A reboot of the BMC on the 6.18 kernel no longer reads as a cold boot.** The daemon logged `cold boot: restoring persisted power state` on every start, with all four modules powered and running, where on the 6.12 kernel it logged `adopted live power state`. <small class="tp-tag">bmcd 2.46.0</small>

    <details class="note">
    <summary>The whole entry</summary>

    The state was restored from the database instead of adopted, so a node that was off in the database but on in fact was switched off. Two causes. The node enable lines were requested as outputs with no value, which drives them to 0; the 6.18 gpio-latch driver writes that into the latch and its shadow, so the read that followed saw "all off" (the 6.12 driver ignored the request). And the enable lines alone cannot tell a running board from a cold one: they are active low, and the bootloader clears every latch on a cold boot, which reads as "enabled". The daemon now reads the boot cookie and the latch shadow word that the bootloader and the kernel keep in SoC SRAM (read-only, through `/dev/mem`, no line is touched) and counts a node as powered when the ATX latch is set and its enable latch is asserted. A cold boot clears the ATX latch, so it still restores the persisted state. The enable lines are requested with what the board is doing, so starting the daemon no longer holds running modules in reset for a moment. Where the shadow cannot be read, and on a v2.4 board, the daemon reads the enable lines as before.

    </details>

- **A confirm window that never starts is no longer a trap.** The window starts when the uplink forwards. <small class="tp-tag">bmcd 2.45.0</small>

    <details class="note">
    <summary>The whole entry</summary>

    With LACP that is easy to miss (an uncabled port, a switch with no LACP on the other end, a bond that never negotiates), and the board was then off the network with nothing to put it back until a power cycle. A change whose uplink has not forwarded within `window_s` plus 60 seconds of the apply is now put back anyway. (review of #50)

    </details>

- **A failed LACP apply no longer takes down a bond it did not make.** A `bond0` that already exists and is not the running document's (a hand-written file, an earlier run) refuses the apply with a 409 and changes nothing. <small class="tp-tag">bmcd 2.45.0</small>

    <details class="note">
    <summary>The whole entry</summary>

    A failed apply undoes only what it did: the bond is deleted only if this apply created it. (review of #50)

    </details>

- **Boot heal checks the kernel, not just the file.** When `interfaces` matches the confirmed document, heal now also checks that `bond0` exists for LACP, that `br0` has `vlan_filtering` as the document says, and that each front port holds its VLANs, and runs the `post-up` plan once if they are missing. <small class="tp-tag">bmcd 2.45.0</small>

    <details class="note">
    <summary>The whole entry</summary>

    A `br0` whose DHCP failed before `post-up` ran no longer leaves bmcd believing in VLANs that were never programmed. A hand-written `interfaces` is still left alone, and a `bond0` it made is not made again. (review of #50)

    </details>

- **A revert does not stop at the first error, and reports what is there.** The timer and a requested revert now try every step even if one fails (a half-moved ge0 and ge1 left the board stranded), and the document reported as running is then read back from the kernel instead of assumed. <small class="tp-tag">bmcd 2.45.0</small>

    <details class="note">
    <summary>The whole entry</summary>

    (review of #50)

    </details>

- **`post-up` lines that delete the bridge's own VLAN end in `|| true`.** `ifupdown` stops at a `post-up` that fails, and the delete of an entry that may not exist must not cancel the lines after it. <small class="tp-tag">bmcd 2.45.0</small>
- **Confirm reads the clock once.** The check and the commit used two readings; if the window ended while the two files were being flushed, the commit refused after both were written, and the timer then put the old document back under a configuration nobody confirmed. <small class="tp-tag">bmcd 2.45.0</small>

    <details class="note">
    <summary>The whole entry</summary>

    (review of #50)

    </details>

- **The preview warnings for a LACP document ask about `bond0`,.** Not about ge0 and ge1, which are empty slaves by design. <small class="tp-tag">bmcd 2.45.0</small>
