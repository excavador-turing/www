The board now runs the mainline Linux 6.18 kernel, and the Realtek switch is driven by the driver that ships in it. The EEPROM and the clock chip share two pins with the switch and now take turns safely, and an update rolls itself back if the board loses its gateway. Thanks to [@danste22](https://github.com/danste22) (Daniel) for the kernel port ([#85](https://github.com/excavador-turing/BMC-Firmware/pull/85)), building on upstream work by Sven Rademakers and Sam Edwards.

<!-- more -->

Firmware **v2.51.0**, 20 November 2026. Every item below is the repository's own changelog entry, taken apart; the whole entry is a click away under each one.

## New

### The promotion gate also requires `ge0` and `ge1`, a readable EEPROM and a bound RTC driver

After a new image boots, the switch, the EEPROM and the RTC now share two pins, so a broken hand-over between them has to stop the update.

<details class="note">
<summary>The whole entry</summary>

Interfaces are checked for existence, never for carrier: a board with an uplink unplugged still passes.

</details>

### The promotion gate also requires the default gateway to answer again, if it answered when the update was staged

`osupdate` (so both `tpi-selfupdate` and an upload through the web interface) now runs `/sbin/stage-gateway-note`, which records the default gateway and whether it answered a ping in `/mnt/overlay/staged-gateway`.

<details class="note">
<summary>The whole entry</summary>

On the tentative boot the gate retries for up to 60 s and rolls back if the gateway stays silent, so a switch driver that probes but carries no traffic can no longer make a board permanent and unreachable. A board with no gateway, or one that never answered, is not blocked. The check needs the staging tools of the image that is *running*, so it first protects the update after the one that installs it.

</details>


## Changed

- **Linux 6.18.63 LTS and the mainline rtl8365mb switch driver over SMI, by
@danste22 (#85), thanks!** The kernel moves from 6.12.109 to 6.18.63.

    <details class="note">
    <summary>The whole entry</summary>

    The Realtek switch is now driven by the mainline `rtl8365mb` code (VLAN, FDB and bridge offload, backported commit by commit) on a GPIO SMI bus, and a new arbiter, written by @danste22, shares the two pins with the EEPROM and the RTC. The node power rails still hold across a BMC reboot through a rewritten `preserve-boot-state` patch. The default flat `br0` behaviour is unchanged. It ran on a board for 30 minutes as v2.51.0-rc.1 before this release. The hardware LAG patches of #79 are not part of it and follow in a separate change.

    Credit where it is due: the preserve-boot-state patch builds on work by Sven
    Rademakers, and the RTL8370MB-CG chip row, the mv64xxx bus-error fix and the
    Turing Pi 2 GPIO aggregator compatible on work by Sam Edwards, all from
    turing-machines/BMC-Firmware; the PWM fan driver is Aleksandr Shubin's and
    the switch driver is mainline Linux by its authors, Alvin Šipraga and Luiz
    Angelo Daros de Luca. Copyright lines are kept.

    </details>
